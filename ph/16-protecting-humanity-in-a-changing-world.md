# Lecture 20: Protecting humanity in a changing world

## Rules to know cold

1. AI and IHRL: privacy (mass surveillance, data processing; Zornetta and Cofone), discrimination (biased datasets, gender; Lutz), disinformation and threats to democracy, incitement, and the environmental footprint. Positive obligations of States to regulate (EU AI Act; the CoE Framework Convention on AI, 2024).
2. AI and IHL: autonomous weapons systems (AWS); the debate on 'meaningful human control' (the GGE under the CCW; UNGA resolutions 2023–24). API Art 36 legal review of new weapons. Can AI apply distinction, proportionality and precautions? Responsibility gaps (who is criminally liable?). Decision-support targeting (e.g. reported use in Gaza: Lavender, Gospel). The Martens Clause and 'dehumanisation' of the battlefield. Lobo's 'Robocop reimagined': AI may improve IHL compliance.
3. AI and ICL: documentation, open-source investigation (OSINT; the Berkeley Protocol), evidence authentication versus deepfakes, and bias. AI as a tool and as a challenge.
4. Climate crisis: Billy v Australia (HRC 2022, Arts 17 and 27); Sacchi (CRC, extraterritorial jurisdiction); KlimaSeniorinnen v Switzerland (ECtHR 2024, Art 8 positive obligation to mitigate); IACtHR OC-32/25 (the climate emergency, a right to a healthy climate, Global South perspectives, per Auz); ICJ Climate AO (July 2025: obligations under treaties and custom, due diligence, a stringent standard, possible State responsibility, a human right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment).
5. Climate and armed conflict: environmental harm in war (API 35(3), 55: widespread, long-term and severe damage; Rome 8(2)(b)(iv) environmental damage; the ILC PERAC principles 2022); conflict as a climate driver; displacement.
6. Humanity beyond Earth: the Outer Space Treaty (space as the 'province of all mankind'), astronauts as 'envoys of mankind', military uses of space, and the Moon Agreement's 'common heritage of mankind'.
7. Throughline: IL's protective architecture is being tested (technology, climate, geopolitics, backlash against courts), so the course's synthesis is co-application and interaction of IHRL, IHL and ICL as mutually reinforcing.
8. ICJ Climate AO (23 July 2025, unanimous), key paragraphs: 1.5°C as the primary goal (224); NDCs must collectively make 1.5°C achievable, with limited discretion (245–249); stringent due diligence (246, 254); duty to cooperate (301); customary obligations bind non-parties (315); right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment inherent in other rights (393); breach may result from fossil-fuel production, subsidies or licensing; erga omnes (partes) invocation (440, 443); causation two-step (437). KlimaSeniorinnen execution: the Committee of Ministers (Mar and Sept 2025) kept Switzerland under supervision and asked for a further report in 2026.
9. Autonomous weapons, latest: UNGA Res 80/57 (1 Dec 2025, 164–6–7). The CCW GGE finished its mandate with 'elements of an instrument'. At the CCW Seventh Review Conference (Geneva, 16–21 Nov 2026) the word 'negotiate' was blocked. States agreed only to 'elaborate' a possible instrument over two years (to 2028), with no commitment to binding rules. The UNSG and ICRC call for a binding instrument on meaningful human control.
10. Apply Meron's caution to autonomous weapons: the Martens Clause 'does not allow one to build castles of sand' and cannot alone outlaw a contested weapon. Additional prohibitions are 'better attained' through distinction, proportionality and unnecessary suffering, because governments are not ready to turn broad principles of humanity into binding law. So an essay on AWS should build its case on API Art 36 review, precautions and accountability, using the Martens Clause and 'public conscience' as support for a new instrument rather than as an existing ban.

## Cases and materials

### Verein KlimaSeniorinnen v Switzerland (GC)
*ECtHR · 2024*

Art 8 includes a right to effective protection from serious adverse effects of climate change. Switzerland failed to set a carbon budget and meet targets. The association had standing; the individual applicants lacked victim status.

**Use it for:** Climate positive obligations; victim status.

### Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change AO
*ICJ · 2025*

States have stringent due diligence obligations to prevent significant harm to the climate system under treaties and custom. Breach may entail State responsibility. A clean, healthy and sustainable environment is a precondition for human rights.

**Use it for:** Linking climate, IHRL and State responsibility.

### Billy et al. v Australia
*UN HRC · 2022*

Inadequate adaptation measures violated the rights to private and family life and to minority culture of Torres Strait Islanders.

**Use it for:** Climate and IHRL.

### Greenpeace Nordic and Others v Norway
*ECtHR (Chamber) · 2025*

28 Oct 2025: no violation of Art 8 in the 2016 Arctic oil licensing, but there is a sufficiently close link between petroleum licensing and Art 8 climate risks. Before approving production, States must carry out a timely, comprehensive EIA covering combustion (scope 3) emissions. Only the NGOs had standing.

**Use it for:** Procedural climate duties after KlimaSeniorinnen; fossil-fuel extraction.

### Climate Emergency and Human Rights, Advisory Opinion OC-32/25
*IACtHR · 2025*

Adopted 29 May, published 3 July 2025. Autonomous right to a healthy climate; nature as a subject of rights; the prohibition of irreversible environmental and climate damage as jus cogens (majority, three dissents); enhanced due diligence on mitigation, adaptation and regulation of business; intergenerational equity; protection of environmental defenders.

**Use it for:** Regional climate rights doctrine; compare the ICJ AO and KlimaSeniorinnen.

## Test yourself

<details><summary>Open the questions (answers are folded under each)</summary>

**Q. What is 'meaningful human control', and why does it matter for IHL?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The idea that humans must retain sufficient judgment and control over the use of force by weapon systems, to ensure compliance with distinction, proportionality and precautions, and to preserve accountability. The legal anchors are API Art 36, the Martens Clause, and individual criminal responsibility.

</details>

**Q. Name three ways AI challenges the protection of humanity.**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

IHRL: surveillance, discrimination, disinformation. IHL: autonomous targeting and responsibility gaps. ICL: evidence integrity (deepfakes) and attributing intent. AI also offers opportunities: OSINT documentation and compliance support.

</details>

**Q. What are the key climate human rights cases?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Billy v Australia (HRC 2022), Sacchi (CRC 2021), KlimaSeniorinnen (ECtHR 2024), IACtHR OC-23/17 and OC-32/25, and the ICJ Climate AO (2025).

</details>

**Q. How would you analyse an AI-assisted strike that killed civilians because of outdated data?**

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Distinction (was the target a lawful military objective or DPH?), precautions in attack (API 57(2)(a)(i): do everything feasible to verify; a 'brief human review' and outdated data suggest failure), proportionality (expected harm based on reasonably available information), the Martens Clause and meaningful human control; individual responsibility (Art 8(2)(b)(i), (iv) requires intent or knowledge) and command responsibility for system design and use.

</details>
