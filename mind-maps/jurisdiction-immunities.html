Public International Law · Week 8 · Jurisdiction and immunities

Jurisdiction and immunities

When a State may make, apply and enforce its law beyond its borders, and when States, their property and their officials are shielded from foreign courts. Tap any topic for the rule, the authorities and the key quote.

Answering a problem question

Take each measure (arrest, trial, seizure) in this order.

  1. Which jurisdiction?

    Prescriptive, adjudicative or enforcement. Enforcement on another State's territory needs consent (Lotus).

  2. Is there a basis?

    Territoriality first (objective, effects), then nationality, passive personality, the protective principle, universality (treaty or custom). Say who bears the burden.

  3. Is the defendant the State?

    Organ or agency? Sovereign or commercial act by its nature? An exception (Arts 10–12)? A waiver?

  4. Is it an official?

    Troika in office: personal immunity, absolute. Otherwise functional immunity for official acts: argue the crimes exception (Pinochet, ILC draft art 6 [7]) and the territorial exception.

  5. Execution

    Is the property used for sovereign purposes, an embassy account, or central bank property? Seizure is the hardest step.

  6. Alternatives

    Arrest Warrant para 61's four avenues, and international courts under Art 27 Rome Statute.

Paragraphs worth citing

Lotus pp 18–19
Enforcement is territorial; prescription has a wide margin.
Arrest Warrant paras 51–61
Troika immunity; no crimes exception; immunity is not impunity.
Germany v Italy paras 92–97
Immunity is procedural; no jus cogens exception.
Pinochet (No 3)
No functional immunity for CAT torture.
Jordan Referral re Al-Bashir paras 113–115
No Head-of-State immunity before international courts.
Intel para 49
Qualified effects test.
Philippine Embassy (1977)
Mixed embassy accounts immune from execution.
Belgium v Senegal (2012)
Extradite or prosecute under CAT Art 7.
