Public International Law · Week 7 · Jus ad bellum

The prohibition of the use of force

Art 2(4) at the centre: where the rule comes from, what it covers, the two Charter exceptions, consent, the contested claims and what follows a breach. Tap any topic for the rule, the authorities and the key quote.

Answering a problem question

Work through each act in this order.

  1. Map the facts

    List each act, who did it and when. Separate incidents need separate answers.

  2. Attribute

    State organs (ARSIWA Art 4) or instructions and effective control (Art 8; Nicaragua para 115). For an armed attack by a group: sending or substantial involvement (Res 3314 Art 3(g); Nicaragua para 195).

  3. Breach

    Armed force in international relations, or a threat of it? If not, consider coercive intervention or a violation of sovereignty. Cite Art 2(4) and custom.

  4. Justify

    Consent (valid, within limits, not withdrawn), then Chapter VII authorisation (express, within scope), then self-defence: armed attack, attacker, timing, necessity, proportionality, report, request. Then assess any other claim.

  5. Consequences

    Cessation and reparation; for a serious breach, non-recognition and non-assistance (ARSIWA Art 41); no territorial gains; the crime of aggression for leaders (Art 8 bis and ICC jurisdiction).

  6. Conclude

    A clear answer for each act, after the other side's best argument.

Paragraphs worth citing

Nicaragua para 176
Self-defence exists in custom alongside Art 51; necessity and proportionality come from custom.
Nicaragua para 186
Breaches treated as breaches confirm the rule.
Nicaragua paras 188–190
Opinio juris from Res 2625; the prohibition as custom and jus cogens.
Nicaragua para 191
Armed attack = the 'most grave forms' of force.
Nicaragua para 195
Scale and effects; armed bands; arms supply is not an armed attack.
Nicaragua paras 199–200
Request for collective self-defence; reporting as evidence.
Nicaragua paras 205, 228
Coercion as the essence of intervention; arming and training rebels is force, funding is intervention.
Corfu Channel p 35
The alleged right of intervention is 'the manifestation of a policy of force'.
Nuclear Weapons AO paras 41, 47
Necessity and proportionality whatever the weapon; a threat is unlawful if the force would be.
Oil Platforms paras 57, 64, 72, 76–77
Burden of proof; 'specifically aimed'; accumulation; a single warship; necessity of the targets.
Wall AO para 139
Armed attack 'by one State against another State'; threats from occupied territory.
DRC v Uganda paras 45–53
Consent: its limits and its withdrawal.
DRC v Uganda paras 146–148
Non-attributable attacks; disproportionate reach; no force for 'perceived security interests'.
