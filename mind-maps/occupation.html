The law of occupation

When territory is occupied, what the occupant may and must do, how the population is protected, settlements and annexation, security measures, human rights, and the legality of a prolonged occupation. Tap any topic for the rule, the authorities and the key quote.

Answering a problem question

Work through an occupation scenario in this order.

  1. Is it occupied?

    Effective control without consent (Art 42; DRC v Uganda para 173; Naletilić); which areas exactly?

  2. Still occupied?

    After withdrawal, apply the functional approach: which powers does the occupant keep (OPT AO paras 91–94)?

  3. The occupant's duties

    Public order and civil life, respecting local law (Art 43); public property as usufructuary (Art 55).

  4. Protections engaged

    Match the facts to GCIV: transfers and deportations, collective punishment, destruction, relief, settlements.

  5. Security measures

    Internment only for imperative security reasons, with review (Art 78); law enforcement standards for policing.

  6. Legality and others' duties

    Annexation or permanent control? Self-determination? Then non-recognition and non-assistance by third States.
